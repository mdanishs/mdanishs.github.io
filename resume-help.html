Resume help

You’re not underqualified. You’re underrepresented.

I’m a senior software engineer and a CDI Certified Resume Analyst. I help engineers moving to Canada and the United States land interviews — by fixing how their experience reads, not by telling them to apply more.

The short version

Most engineers who moved to North America are rejected for how their experience is presented, not for the experience itself. North American recruiters screen for business impact — scale, ownership, architecture decisions, and measurable outcomes — while most resumes written elsewhere list responsibilities and tenure. Fixing that gap is the fastest lever available, and it does not require new skills, new certifications, or more applications.

Who this is for

Internationally trained software engineers making the move to Canada or the United States.

You have real, senior-level skills. And you are watching those skills get discounted — “Canadian experience” gatekeeping, credentials that don’t translate, a professional network you have to rebuild from zero, and applicant tracking system rejections despite being genuinely good at the work.

The people I help most are the ones who have already landed and stalled: months of applications, no callbacks, no idea what’s wrong. That’s the case where repositioning changes things fastest.

What actually changes when the positioning is fixed

Candidates who had spent months hearing nothing started averaging around three interview invitations per week after repositioning.

Nothing about their experience changed. Nothing about their technical ability changed. The only thing that changed was how they presented themselves.

Your resume isn’t just a document. It’s the first version of your professional story that every recruiter reads. If that story undersells your experience, your application ends before your skills ever get a chance to speak.

The four things we fix

01

Impact over responsibilities

“Responsible for the checkout flow” becomes what it actually was: the traffic it carried, the revenue it moved, the decision you owned.

02

Seniority that reads as seniority

Engineering experience positioned using scale, architecture, ownership, and measurable outcomes — the four signals North American hiring managers screen on.

03

Survivable by the ATS

Structure and language that make it through applicant tracking systems intact, without keyword-stuffing your way into sounding fake.

04

A story that holds up in the room

The resume sets up the interview. Everything on it is something you can defend, expand on, and be proud of when someone asks.
