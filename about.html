About

The engineering lane

I’ve been building software professionally for over {{ site.brand.years-experience }} years. Most of that time has been in the frontend — React, React Native, Next.js, TypeScript — with enough backend work in Node.js, GraphQL, and Python that I’ve never needed to hand a feature off halfway.

Reusable components that other teams actually adopt. Workshops that make a junior engineer dangerous in a good way.

I like fast-paced environments where I can ship production-quality code quickly and watch it land. See the full experience →

The lane I didn’t plan on

I moved to Canada as an experienced engineer and learned something uncomfortable: strong skills don’t automatically translate. The North American market reads resumes differently than services markets do, and nobody tells you that on the way in. You just apply, and apply, and hear nothing.

I figured it out for myself, then started helping other people figure it out. That turned into a CDI Certified Resume Analyst credential, then {{ site.brand.resumes-reviewed }}+ resume reviews for internationally trained engineers stuck in the same loop, then HireDoc — an interview-based tool that pulls real impact out of your work instead of paraphrasing what’s already on the page.

It’s the same problem as my day job, honestly. Good work is worthless if the person evaluating it can’t see it. More on how I help →

Outside the work

I’m originally from Karachi, Pakistan, and studied at the National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences. I started out building games — Unity, Cocos2D, LibGDX — and won a handful of hackathons and game jams along the way, including Google’s Develop for Good in the green category. That’s still where the instinct to just build the thing comes from.

I read a lot, mostly about craft, confidence, and how businesses actually work. I write about both of my lanes on LinkedIn, where {{ site.brand.resumes-reviewed }}+ resume reviews have turned into a fairly opinionated body of work on the North American job search.
